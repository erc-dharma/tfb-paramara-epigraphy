PennGlobal: PMR185A
Period: Lost
Location: Dhār
King: Vindhyavarman/ Subhaṭavarman
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Metrical
Metres: All in Anuṣṭhubh 
Gist: Records the donation of two gardens by the same king, 
	ie Subhaṭavarman to Bilhana, who was his sāndhivigrahiha, 
	ie Minister for peace and war.
Published reference: Trivedi (1989), no. 185A.

{1} From Lele's transcript in A. B. O. I., Vol. XI, pp. 49 ff. It may be noted here that his transcript 
	does not represent the original accurately and he has made several changes, e. g., that v to b, as is
	obvious.
{2} For the sake of convenience, the verse existing on the extant portion has been taken here as 1. Its
	metre is not certain; and the portion commencing the second line appears to be a part of either
	Mandākrāntā or Śragdharā.
{3} I am not certain about the metre in this line, which is fragmentary.
{4} Insert a daṇḍa here.
{5} Here the number marking the stanza is put, of course presuming that two stanzas are fragmentary on 
	the preceding portion which is now not available. 
{6} This verse has lost 14 letters of the first quarter, the whole of the second quarter, and three letters of 
	the third.
{7} Read -rhṛdikṛtaśrī-.
{8} Read a visarga after this akṣara.
{9} Here this akṣara has to be taken separately in the padacheda.
{10} Read bhayaṃ.
{11} Lele has taken this line separate but it appears to be connected with the preceding one and in con-
	 tinuation of it.
{12} This akṣara should be prosodically long. Should we read mō with the case ending? 
{13} We should read here either sadyaḥ, or adya, dropping the visarga.
{14} It is incorrect as it should be -nṛta-, but it cannot be known whether it is a mistake in the original
	 or in the transcript.
{15} This verse is separate from the preceding one and is only conjectural from Lele's transcript. It is not
	 possible to know if some more akṣaras are lost here. 
{16} One long letter is missing after da.
{17} Prosodically, the bracketed letter should be long. 
{18} Read me'ghaṃ.
{19} Sandhi is violated here. 
{20} As the stone is broken on both the vertical sides, it is not possible to ascertain as to how many of 
	 these letters are lost at the end of this line and how many at the beginning of the next line. This
	 is not to be noted in case of the other breaks also. 
{21} Read sakhaśriyāṃ.
{22} Probably the date was given in the end. The break in this verse may perhaps indicate that the gift
	 was made by the king Subhaṭavarman, whose name was possibly shown, for metrical exigencies, by 
	 the letters Suvarmaṇā just at the end of the first hemistich of the stanza. This is also justi-
	 fied by taking vindhyavarmatanayena as one compound word, taken as two by Lele, which cannot be construed
	 properly.
