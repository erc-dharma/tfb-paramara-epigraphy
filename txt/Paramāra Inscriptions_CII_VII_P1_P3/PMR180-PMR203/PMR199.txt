{1} From the original and an impression.
{2} Expressed, respectively, by symbol, and the Nāgarī letter u with the sign of anusvāra above.
{3} Here, and at the end of some other lines below, there is an unnecessary daṇḍa followed by a horizontal
	stroke, intended to cover a little blank space at its end.
{4} The daṇḍa is placed quite close to the preceding akṣara; so as to appear as the sign of mātrā.
{5} The anusvāra on lli is redundant.
{6} These three akṣaras are mutilated beyond recognition, and the traces show that they may have been
	ranumī.
{7} This akṣara appears as bhau, followed by a daṇḍa; but it gives no meaning.
{8} The fourth akṣara in this foot is required to be long. Probably we have to read it as rṣa.
{9} Should we read it as stotraṃ nu ?
{10} Both these akṣaras are mutilated beyond recognition. The lacune may be filled in by reading
	 buddhya. 
{11} It is doubtful whether it is the sign of visarga, or jihvāmūlīya.
{12} The marks show that all these letters are either mutilated or misformed. They cannot be made out.
{13} The reading of this singular akṣara in this line is not certain. It is followed by a flowery design
	 and a double daṇḍa as marked here, but the inscription is incomplete, as already stated above.
