Period: VS 1366 śrāvaṇa vadi 12
Location: Udaipur
King: Jayasimhadeva
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Prose 
Gist: The inscription records the donation of one-eighth of the revenue 
	from the village Hathivāha by the Thakura. 
	Vidyādhara to a Brāhmaṇa.
Published reference: Trivedi (1989), no. 186.


Text{1}

{1} From the original stone. Subsequently, I have also compared my reading from the facsimile facing p. 35
	of Ep. Ind., Vol. XXXVIII. The inscription begins with a symbol which is partly mutilated. 
{2} Here supply virājamāna, or mālālaṃkṛta, or, as suggested by Ramsharma, sahita; but on the original
	it appears, as taken here, at the beginning of the third line. 
{3} The reading of the bracketed akṣaras is based on lose ending the name of a village which exists in 
	the neighbourhood even today. The mātrā of the first of them appears to be mixed with the slanting 
	stroke of the second. On the original, the last two syllables appear as kapā. 
{4} This akṣara is followed by two hollow circles and a horizontal stroke, and the reading of them, as 
	adopted here, is doubtful. Ramasharma read the fourth and the fifth letters in this line as satka. 
{5} This akṣara is deformed, and the mātrā on the preceding re resembles a curve. 
{6} Two or three akṣaras are lost at the end of the line, and the reading tasī is adopted here from that
	of Ramsharma; but the reading is not certain, as the last of the akṣaras appears as a conjunct.
{7} Read prakṣālyātra. Possibly, what is intended is udakaiḥ, for (udakaṃ ?).
{8} Two or three akṣaras are lost here also.
{9} Some letters are indistinct here, and some are lost at the end. Ramsharma read them as [ā][jñā*](nti)
	ṭha vaijja (?). He also took vaijja engraved for vaidya; but I do not find the ending certain. 
{10} Eight akṣaras are lost here and they were read by Ramsharma as śrīcaṃdraprabha[pra*]ma(mā)ṇa, with some doubt
	 from an impression prepared in 1961-62.
