PennGlobal: PMR200
Period: Undated
Location: Sāwargāon
King: Jagaddēva
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Metrical
Metres: Śragdharā.  
Gist: Refers to an illustrious hero of the name of Jagaddēva who is described as vanquishing  thousans of (enemical)  warriors, 
	all alone, when those who sided with him fled. The inscription describes the fight. 
	Enemy likely to have been none other than the Hoysala Ballāla.
Published reference: Trivedi (1989), no. 200.



Text{1}


1. svasthi{2} | satrāse kālidāse{3} drava-
2. ti vigalati kvāpi karṇṇāṭada[ṇṭe]{4}
3. pāṇḍaye kudhasya{5} raṃgkraṃ paṭati bhaṭi-
4. ti ca bhramyati svepi sainye [|]{6} bhānau{7}
5. pūrvvādrisānau bhramati bhujava(ba)laḥ{8} śrī-
6. jagaddevadevo lakṣyaṃ{9} vīraḥ pravīra [ḥ]
7. [a]jayadiha{10} dṛḍhaḥ kālameghādhirū-
8. [ḍhaḥ]ḥ{11} || aśva[tthā]makaveḥ{12} ślokaḥ |

-------------------------------------------------

{1} From photograph facing page 144 of Vidarbha Research Society's Annual (Marathi), for 1973.
{2} This word is proceded by some indistinct incisions appearing as of the Siddham symbol.
{3} The poet is very fond of using alliterations as he has done here and also below, for example, in
	dravati and vigalati, kudhra and randhra, bhānau and sānau, etc.
{4} The bracketed akṣara is not well formed, but the reading is certain. The word daṇḍa means an
	army here.
{5} The word which is used here for alliteration means a mountain.
{6} The daṇḍa has disappeared, leaving only traces.
{7} As said above, here the word denotes Vikramāditya VI of the Western Chalūkya dynasty.
{8} The word bala is used in double meaning: army and strength. SInce Jagaddeva's army had scattered,
	he won the battle on his own strength.
{9} Prof. Kolte emends the second letter to kṣya, meaning the aim. But it is unnecessary. Here it 
	means hundred thousands, that is, innumerable, which is also consistent with ajayat.
{10} The first akṣara in this line is partly broken, and the sandhi is not observed for the sake of metre.
{11} This akṣara too is partly broken. Kālamegha, literally meaning 'cloud of the deluge', appears as the 
	 name of Jaggadeva's horse.
{12} Kolte read the third letter of the name as ccha and corrected it to stha. But also see the same
	 letter in the name of the poet, above, on Plate XXXII, line 28.
