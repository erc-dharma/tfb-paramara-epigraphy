PennGlobal: PMR188
Period: VS 1314 phālguṇa vadi 0 (amāvāsyāṃ)
Location: Chītrī
Language: Sanskrit with local dialect
Script: Nagari
Text: Prose  
Gist: Records the restoration of the temple where it was found. 
	It is stated to have been originally constructed by Sanatkumāra, 
	who belonged to the Paramāra clan, and was restored by the Rāūta Jāla.
Published reference: Trivedi (1989), no. 188.


Text{1}

1. siddham{2} | saṃ. 1314 varṣe{3} phālguṇa vadi ൦ dhu(bu)dha-
2. dine śrīsātalesva(śva)rasatkaprāsāda[ḥ]{4} rā-.{5}
3. jālena uddhṛtaḥ || paramāravaṃ[sa(śa)]saṃ-
4. gha--{6}rāṇa(?)nsaṃtakumāreṇa kī[ya ?] si(śi)-

------------------------------------------------
{1} From impressions.
{2} Expressed by a symbol. 
{3} After the first two figures of the year, there appears to be a scratch resembling the symbol for 4. There 
	is also a scratch appearing as the sign of mātrā on the following ṇa, or the sign of mātrā on this 
	letter, which was first engraved and later on erased (?).
{4} The sign of visarga has disappeared, leaving traces. The letter that precedes this sign is deformed,
	also appearing as va or dha. 
{5} That is Rāūta.
{6} The reading of the first akṣara in this line is not certain; the second is indistinct, and the third one
	has a vertical stroke on either side, appearing as the sign of either a pṛṣṭha mātrā, or of ā. Thus 
	the reading of the first four letters in this line is uncertain. 
{7} This akṣara, which may have been intended to be ōṃ, as it appears also, is redundant.
{8} This akṣara also appears to have attached to it a mātrā, which was probably subsequently erased, but 
	the sign is still there.
