PennGlobal: PMR201
Period: VS 1060
Location: Digōdā
King: Vidyādhara
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Prose
Gist: Inscription of great value of Chandēlla rulers and the Turkish invasion of India. 
	The purpose of the inscription is to record a grant to some Brāhmaṇās the (produce of the) village Isaunī, situated in the Vāraṇgi-84.
Published reference: Trivedi (1989), no. 201.

{1} From the original plate and impression.
{2} Expressed by a symbol represented by the Nāgarī ū with the sign of anusvāra above.
{3} The upper circle of the visarga is engraved as the Nāgarī ō and the lower is omitted. The names
	of Chandēlla kings, also end in Brahma in the Pṛthvīrāja Rāsō (Nāgarī Pracāriṇī Sabha), pp.25-28.
{4} For the reading of the consonant in gō, see the same in Gaṇḍa in line 2, and for that of tvā, see the
	same letter in datvā in line 6. The second appear to be a strange name. The sense of Chāha above.,
	is not clear to me, but the reading is certain. Probably vin=atra is intended (?).
{5} Sandhi is not made here and also in the next line in ddha and ī.
{6} What appears to have been intended is kṛśānavē lōvam. The punctuation mark is redundant. 
{7} What seems as the sign of anusvāra on ra in the impression is due to the fault of the plate, as seen from
	the original.
{8} This verb goes with mālā in line 4, which, however, cannot grammatically be connected with the word
	Satyabhāmā nor also with tayā. Moreover, after this verb, one expects an expression like yathā. 
{9} That is, Ṭhakkura. The next akṣara may also have been a misformed ja, and thus its reading is 
	not certain. Most of the names and some other words in this and the following lines are without case-
	endings and are also unnecessarily separated by punctuation marks. The corrections are not made every
	time lest the number of footnotes should increase. The first akṣara of the name is blurred and also 
	seems as ma. It may also be noted that in the following lines some details with reference to the donees
	are dropped, and in some instances, nothing besides the names is found, as already stated.
{10} The numeral 3 probably represents the pravaras, or it may have been a misformed ya, as required here.
{11} The consonant of the first akṣara of the name of the place may also be read as r or v.
{12} All the akṣaras of the name after tathā are blurred and their reading is uncertain.
{13} Grammatically it is incorrect.
{14} From the construction it is not possible to know anything about the names of the brothers, here and 
	 in some cases below. Perhaps Nimvāka and his brothers (?).
{15} An arrow mark above this akṣara probably indicates that the line in the left margin has to be supplied
	 here, but the corresponding mark is not engraved.
{16} The consonant of the preceding akṣara appears as l.
{17} The reading of the bracketed akṣara is doubtful, and it is not known whether the mātrā of the third
	 akṣara of the name is of ē. One naturally expects the name of the village here.
{18} In this line, and also in some others below, the use of both the words pada and padaka occurs side by 
	 side.
{19} This gōtra seems to refer to those mentioned previously. The letter tra is also written as ttra in this 
	 line.
{20} After an indistinct akṣara following tathā, a portion of the plate is lost in this and the rest of the 
	 lines.
{21} One syllable after dha is indistinct and cannot be made out. It is engraved as rai, or vai, but what
	 appears to be intended is sya.
{22} Both these akṣaras are partly broken and their reading is not certain. They may also be read as 
	 ddhāma. The first three letters, though not separated by a punctuation mark as in the other cases, denote
	 the names of the donee or his father.
{23} Perhaps a daṇḍa is intended here.
{24} Read śāsanēna pradattam for pradattaḥ. One expects a word line ēvaṃ, before this and also iti, before
	 matvā.
{25} The correct form would be eteṣām upanetavyam.
{26} For bhuktā, as usually found.
{27} The use of the numeral showing repetition is noteworthy. Also see above, p.319, Text line 3.
{28} The akṣaras in this line are slightly advanced in form than those in the main inscription. After 
	 Jālha, two of them are altogether lost, and the third one is indistinct. The reading of the number
	 after pada is also uncertain.
{29} The letters which are indistinct on the plate are shown here by the number of dots, and when more,
	 by a line. The second line begins after some space in the first, and ends much before it.
{30} Reading from the original. The akṣaras here are most slovenly formed and those in the brackets 
	 are also weather-worn, but are faintly visible.
{31} Both the numbers in the brackets are almost lost, and their reading is from the traces left.
