Period: 1181 jyeṣṭha vadi 12
Location: Girvad
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Prose and Metrical in 7 verses
Metres: Verses 1-2 Vasantatilakā; v.3 Śārdūlavikrīḍita; vv. 4 and 6-7 Anuṣṭhubh; v.5 Mandākrāntā  
Gist: To record that the temple in which the stone is set up was repaired by one Śrī Satyātmā, after taking permission 
from Griviḍa (Girvaḍa) Brāhmaṇas, who too made some donations to the deity.
Published reference: Trivedi (1989), no. 187.

{1} From the original and an impression.
{2} Expressed by a symbol.
{3} The first of the daṇḍas is so close to the preceding akṣara as to look like a mātrā, and the second
	appears to have been inserted subsequently.
{4} The sign of rēpha on the first of the bracketed akṣaras is faint even on the original. The idea of 
	the water of the Ganges flowing out of the kamaṇḍalu recalls that in N. S. C., Canto I, V.48.
{5} The slanting stroke of the subscript of the bracketed akṣara is faintly visible even on the original. 
{6} The sign of anusvāra on the penultimate letter is blurred. The meaning of the word pattikā is not
	known to me. Though not known to lexicons, from the context it appears to denote a territorial division. 
{7} The construction in this verse is not proper, but it shows that in that region araghaṭṭas (water- wheels)
	is used to irrigating fields. The word sētī (khētī ?) is local, meaning cultivation, from kṣetra (?).
	According to M. Williams, sētakī is nadyādi.
{8} The subscript letter of the second akṣara is cut as y, ending in a stroke as of ph. The preceding 
	sentence is in the local language, and I am unable to understand it. The sense shows that
	nēchaka is a local word meaning a garden. 
{9} The reading is certain. It probably means those who observe fast for a month.
{10} Pala is a weight equal to four karṣas. 
{11} The word karṣa appears also in No. 84, v.76. The expression that follows has to be corrected to
	 -pratyekādaśi.
