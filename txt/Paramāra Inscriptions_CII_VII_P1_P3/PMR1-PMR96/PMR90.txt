Metres: Verses 1, 13, 14 Mālinī; vv.2, 5, 6, 30 Śārdūlavikrīḍitā; v.25 Gīti;{2} vv.3, 26, 31-33 Āryā;

	    Mandākrāntā; vv.12, 21 Upajāti;{3} vv.15, 16, 39 Śikhariṇī; v.18 Pṛthvī; v.19 Hariṇī
Gist: A secterian record and states the the foundation of a Jain Temple and 
	  the consecration of an image of Vṛṣabhanātha at Utthūṇaka in the province of Sthalī 
	  by one Bhūṣaṇa during the reign of Vijayarāja.
Published reference: Trivedi (1978), no. 90.

{1}	From impressions. The reading was also verified from personal examination of the record. 
{2} Metre irregular, for which see n.8 on the next page. 
{3} And not Upendravajrā, as taken by Halder. 
{4} Expressed by a symbol which is preceded by what appears to be a symbol for siddham, followed by 
	two daṇḍas. The mātrās and the anusvāra appearing above the line are ornamental. 
{5} At the end of the line there is a symbol resembling the akṣara cha followed by two daṇḍas. 
{6} The wrong form is used here, evidently for metrical exigency. 
{7} The vertical stroke of the left limb of ma is faint in the impressions but it is clear in the facsimile accom-
	panying Halder's article in Ep. Ind., Vol. XXI and also on the stone which I examined. 
{8} Halder corrects it to jjo which is not necessary. 
{9} The ninth akṣara of this foot is unnecessarily stressed by the following pra. but also see the Piṅgala
	Sūtra prahre vā, according to which, it is not an offence. 
{10} The strokes following this letter are redundant. 
{11} The daṇḍa is redundant. 
{12} The impression shows a dot above this letter; it may be a redundant stroke or a mātrā (?). It may 
	 also have been ālokaḥ. 
{13} The daṇḍa is redundant, as also some others below
{14} What is at the end of the line is not a daṇḍa but only a flourish of the mātrā. This foot also involves 
	 yati bhaṅga.
{15} The adjective sat is redundant here. 
{16} Probably here the poet intended to use prabhāvāḍhyaḥ, which would not suit the metre. 
{17} There appear some scratching and over writing on this and the preceding two letters, making the reading 
	 indistinct. Probably -ratiśayaḥ may have been intended. 
{18} There are two redundant daṇḍas at the end of this line. 
{19} There is a redundant stroke here. 
{20} The sign of anusvāra, as also some others below, is represented by a hollow circle marked not over but 
	 in continuation of the letter to which it belongs. 
{21} These four akṣaras are totally mutilated and the space occupied by them shows that one more akṣara 
	 may have originally been at first engraved and deleted later on. Probably what appears to have been 
	 engraved is yaśaḥśreyasī. 
{22} The reading of the first letter of the name is uncertain; it may be sīukā also, as read by Halder. 
{23} Taking the second letter of this foot short, as engraved here, it would have 17 mātrās instead of 18, as 
	 required. The name was sthalī and not sthali, which is used for the metre. 
{24} This akṣara can also be read as du. 
{25} This letter is mutilated and looks like mā but is restored here in view of the appears of the word in the 
	 preceding record. 
{26} The daṇḍa is redundant. Moreover, it is strange to note how Rāvaṇa's behaviour is called good here. 
{27} Halder read the name as stamakaḥ, but to me what he took to be ta appears to be a mātrā. utkīrṇṇā is in 
	 feminine as an adjective of kīrtti, meaning praśasti.
{28} This is followed by a floral design and symbol resembling cha, between a double daṇḍa, as also the 
	 first syllable in the next line. 
{29} These three letters are much mutilated and are restored here from Halder's reading. Perhaps what is 
	 intended is kalikaluṣaṃ. 
{30} The reading of the last foot of the stanza is not free from doubt. Halder reads it as va(kṣye) jāyarudhyānāṃ(?), 
	 and the editor of Ep. Ind. (p.40, n.) suggests that perhaps we have to read mokṣāya rodhyānā-. But what both of 
	 them take to be mo is mutilated, and following it, kṣāya is clear. And the following letter, taken here 
	 as ru may also be read as bha, but the next letter is not ktā to give the reading bhaktānāṃ. The reading 
	 of the last letter too is equally doubtful; but compare the formation of the same word in 1.1 above. 
{31} From here, the stanzas are numbered in the original as 1.2 and so on, and I have retained the same num-
	 bers. But while showing the kind of metre above, they are taken as in continuation. 
{32} Halder read prathamito and suggested its correction to prāthamiko. 
{33} These three akṣaras are written on the left hand margin of II.25 to 22, with the sign of kāka pada before
	 them, but the corresponding sign where they are to be supplied is not engraved.
