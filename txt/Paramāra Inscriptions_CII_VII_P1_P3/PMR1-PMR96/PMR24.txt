{1} From the facsimile accompanying Būhler's article in Ep. Ind., Vol. I, and from an impression supplied by
	The Superintending Archaeologist, Central Circle, Bhopal.
{2} As noted by Būhler, the word ālavāla is here broken into the first two of its letters ending the
	first foot and the rest beginning the second foot of this verse. In the latter half also I find that the
	prefix ā is taken at the end of the third foot, and iva is not in its proper place. The error can be
	corrected by the restoration -vallīvābhāti.
{3} In the facsimile and the impression the bracketed akṣaras have disappeared, leaving traces. But they
	are distinct on the stone from which they have been restored here.
{4} This verse is followed by a floral design between two daṇḍas.
{5} These two akṣaras are totally lost. Read -rākhyaḥ, as suggested by Būhler and as they are clear on the
	stone. They mātrā of the preceding tyā was subsequently inserted.
{6} The reading of these two akṣaras is uncertain. They also appear as bhūmiḥ. On the original the
	reading bhīmiḥ is clear.
{7} The vertical of the mātrā of the second akṣara of the name was subsequently inserted.
{8} Read senā, as suggested by Būhler. The word nagāda (Garuḍa), as used in the last pāda of this verse,
	is quite in consonance with engraving this figure in the land grants of the dynasty.
{9} This daṇḍa is joined to the following letter and the preceding da is disfigured by a redundant chisel
	stroke.
{10} Read khaḍga ūrddhvīkṛto, as suggested by Būhler. The fourth akṣara in this verse may be read as rdvī or rddhvī but
	not thdvī, as adopted in the text in its correct form.
{11} Būhler read the bracketed letter as ta but it is distinctly da on the original; karotīti kṛt; it is changed
	to d, by sandhi.
{12} the bracketed letter is so formed as to appear as to or bho or ven so, as also remarked by Būhler. The
	reading could not be ascertained even from my inspection of the original. The following nka is peculiarly
	formed.
{13} The bracketed letters are all extremely damaged and their restoration is due to Būhler. In my examina-
	 tion of the original I found this reading to be certain.
{14} The reading of this letter is again doubtful. On the original, however, mu is very clear.
{15} The second yati of the third pāda of this verse contains two syllables in excess. The flaw can be removed
	 by deleting bharaṃ. The letters rabha are struck off on the original.
{16} The last letter is doubtfully read here. It is a protion of a verse in Sragdharā, and the following
	 protion is continued on another stone. The restoration of these ten letters is merely from the inked
	 impression and my personal examination of the original stone. They are not distinct in the facsimile, and 
	 were read by Būhler as (kuṃvānya-)tavājivrajaru; but this gives no sense. The suggestion about the reading of
	 the last letter is due to S. L. Katare.
{17} From an impression.
{18} This is in continuation of the last letter engraved on the first part of the praśasti.
{19} The first akṣara of this word has not clearly come out on the impression. It appears as cū, but would
	 make no sense. And the third one bears a redundant stroke, but its reading is absolutely certain.
{20} This letter appears to have been damaged.
{21} What follows is all abraded, as already stated, and consequently is not completely legible. It may also
	 be noted here that the impression is complete, as shown by the last letters, which can be read as -teyaṃ praśastiḥ,
	 followed by a flowery design between a double daṇḍa on either side. It is the last part of a verse
	 giving the name of the writer or the engraver, which too cannot be made out.
