{1} From a photograph.
{2} The verses are numbered as in this plate.
{3} Kielhorn read this akṣara as ka and remarked that it may possibly be read as ke. But I take it to be ke,
	considering that the mātrā has left a trace above the top-stroke. On the word kalpita that follows, see
	n. in No. 12, 1.10.
{4} To be restored to parivarttanena.
{5} The sign of anusvāra is faint but it is there.
{6} Possibly to be read as saṃbaddhe. It is interesting to note that along with nivartana, the standard measure of
	land, the extent of land is also shown here by the use of the measure of hala, one hala signifying as much 
	land as could be ploughed by a single pair of bullocks.
{7} The mātrā is faintly visible.
{8} The va appears as na.
{9} Possibly the sign of anusvāra is also engraved on the letter sa.
{10} This is a contraction of dūtaka.
{11} Kielhorn remarks: "This akṣara, which is engraved on a level with the preceding words but is of much
	 larger size, I am unable to explain properly. I can only suggest that it may stand for racitaṃ(which
	 we find in the grants of Arjunavarman), and that it should have been followed by the name of the 
	 official who executed the grant." To me, however, it appears to be an ornamentation, as already stated
	 above.
{12} I think that the woed adhiśrīḥ is broken into two and is so written for the sake of symmetry.
