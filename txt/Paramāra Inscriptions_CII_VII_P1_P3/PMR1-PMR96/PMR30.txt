{1} From impressions.
{2} Expressed by symbols, the latter of which is engraved above the line just after the mātrā of dhye.
{3} The traces show that the first three of the missing akṣaras were lasat.
{4} Perhaps what is intended is ghaṭanā, meaning 'happening'.
{5} The daṇḍa is redundant, as some others below.
{6} The reading of this letter is doubtful. Its consonant also appears as chh, but the sense is not clear.
{7} As no.5 above.
{8} The lacuna may be filled in as santarjjitārāti by also changing bhāri.
{9} The fifth to the seventh of the letters of the third foot appear to read the name of Sīyaka. The second
	foot has five letters more, some of which appear to have been scored off.
{10} From the context this letter appears to have been kṣmī. This quarter has two letters in excess. Re-
	 store as śrīmāñjayī svīyabhujārjjitaśrīḥ.
{11} The reading of the bracketed akṣara is doubtful. Perhaps what is intended is gaṇānāṃ.
{12} Read -kṛtaḥ prajānāṃ.
{13} The letters are damaged and the reading is not certain.
{14} Read tannirmmale in the sense of tasminnirmmale.
{15} This akṣara is unusually formed but the reading is certain. The reading of the second akṣara of the
	 name of the family is not certain.
{16} This akṣara is preceded by a redundant daṇḍa. Perhaps we have to read prasūtiḥ, but the upper curve of
	 the medial i is misiing.
{17} A pṛṣṭha mātrā was first engraved before dhe and then scored off. The letter that follows is engraved
	 as rcci.
{18} The daṇḍa is redundant.
{19} A conjunct letter is illegible here and the reading of the preceding letter too is doubtful.
{20} For kāritaṃ, for which see Ind. Ant., Vol. XIX, p.62, n.53. Read utkhānitaḥ or nirmāpitaḥ which would also
	 suit the metre.
{21} The daṇḍa is redundant.
{22} The reading of the bracketed letter is doubtful and the fifth letter also appears as se. Probably what
	 the poet means to say is that the water of this pond is as clear as emerald. The construction is defective.
{23} Originally, a medial ā was attached to this letter and it was subsequently scored off as redundant.
{24} Read suvyāyataṭaṃ. A redundant ta, engraved before ya at first, was scored off later on.
{25} The meaning of this word is not clear to me and the second akṣara of the word may also have been mā.
{26} The reading of the bracketed akṣara is uncertain; The first consonant of the first akṣara is s, and that
	 of the second is v, which is preceded by a daṇḍa. May it be śyāma in the sense of the cuckoo? Or, if
	 śyāva, it means dark brown or brown.
{27} Possibly we have to read yacca.
{28} The mātrā of tā appears to have been scored off.
{29} By changing vyanaṣṭe to vinaṣṭe the sense appears to be that 'whosoever rebuilds this pond after it is
	 broken'.
{30} Drop the visarga and read ṭaṃkānāṃ rājamudritānāṃ, which, however, would not suit the metre.
{31} Expressed by a symbol. An ornamental design precedes the daṇḍas.
{32} The missing akṣaras appear to have been ṭaṃkitaṃ.
{33} This word, as shown by the traces that follow, may have some design after it, or they may have been
     only scratches.
