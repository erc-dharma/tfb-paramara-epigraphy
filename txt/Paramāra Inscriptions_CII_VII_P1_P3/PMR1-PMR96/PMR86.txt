{1} From an impression. 
{2} Denoted by a symbol. 
{3} The daṇḍa is redundant. 
{4} The traces following pra appears to be as of tiṣṭhā and all the missing akṣaras here may have been -pratiṣṭhānvitaṃ.
{5} The pṛṣṭha mātrā is detached from this and appears as a daṇḍa. The next letter looks more 
	like Jha. But it may also have been bha. 
{6} This and the preceding sign of visarga were inserted subsequently. It cannot be known whether the mark 
	above is that of anusvāra or a fault of the stone. Following this letter, hā was originally incised and 
	the mātrā appears to have been later on cancelled by an arrowhead below it. 
{7} The lacunae may be filled in by -kāryaparo mānyo nitya sa-, of which the first five letters are in traces. But
	this would not explain the use of kramāt. 
{8} The verticals of the first letter of the name are joined by a horizontal stroke as those of dhā, and the sign 
	of anusvāra on the second is faintly visible. The missing akṣaras may have mentioned something about 
	the king. 
{9} Some of these akṣaras are partly visible, but nothing can be made out of them. 
{10} The portion mentioning the first two figures of the date is lost but these two figures can be restored as 
	 11 with the help of those appearing in the end. Thus, the year is 1159. 
{11} Of the whole quarter which is lost here, it cannot be said how many akṣaras were in this and in the 
	 preceding line, which also shows 4 or 5 akṣaras struck off by horizontal lines. 
{12} The mātrā of the first letter in the brackets appears to have been first wrongly incised and later on struck 
	 off by two horizontal strokes -one on the vertical and the other on the curve above; and the lowest ex-
	 termity of the vertical stroke ending ya is curved to the right instead of to the left. See the same letter 
	 in 1.5 above. 
{13} This akṣara is more like jo(जो) than to(तो); and unfortunately, the second akṣara of a word showing the 
	 relationship is lost. The reading may have been anujo (?).
{14} The reading is certain except that the pṛṣṭha mātrā taken here as of vē may have been that of ā attached 
	 to the preceding letter. This broken part, however, does not give any sense. 
{15} The akṣaras in the brackets are conjecturally restored. The following five akṣaras that are lost are 
	 apparently meant to describe the lady. 
{16} This expression is metrically faulty; read kṛtyacāritra-.
{17} The rest of the inscription is all broken.
