{1} From impressions.
{2} A portion of the slab has peeled off and the bracketed letters which are now missing are supplied from 
	Acharya's reading. 
{3} The eleventh and the thirteenth of the akṣaras of the fourth foot of this verse are damaged; and whereas 
	Acharya read the last word as harśā(rṣā)t, Nahar read it as darśāt, but the sense of the latter half of 
	this verse is not intelligible to me. The second letter of the latter half of this verse is equally damaged. 
{4} The bracketed letter has left only traces and hence the reading is not certain. 
{5} The name of Bharaṇīvarāha, found in the Vasantagaḍh inscription, appears to have been lost here in 
	v.7 or 8. The first hemistich of this verse mentions a king and compares him with Dharaṇīdhara, i.e.,
	Varāha; "and just as the latter supported the dharā (Earth) immersed in Sindhurāja (the ocean), so
	the king also supported the dharā (kingdom) of Sindhurāja", the originator of the family. See Ind. Ant.,
	Vol. XL, p.239.
{6} This akṣara is as read by Acharya, but the metre would not require a conjunct consonant here. In 
	the impressions it has totally disappeared and the preceding letter is also partly visible. 
{7} Only traces of the bracketed letters are visible and they are supplied from Acharya's reading. 
{8} The two akṣaras with the number of the verse are restored from Acharya's reading. The following 
	three akṣaras navate are damaged. 
{9} The mātrā of this akṣara is engraved as if mingled with the vertical of the preceding one. 
{10}  Originally, ye engraved, later on changed to rye.
{11} The akṣaras in rectangular brackets are damaged, leaving only traces. Similarly, the first akṣara of 
	 the preceding name is equally damaged; it is read as ki both by Acharya and Nahar. But it may have 
	 been vi, view of the last letter of the name being ṭa, which is very clear in the impressions. 
{12} Rhythm requires the fourth letter in this verse to be long. 
{13} The second akṣara in the line is corrupt and may be read as ta(त),na(न) or even va. In view of this, the 
	 reading appears to be either śveta or ścena, to be restored to ścaitat. But even with this, I am unable
	 to make out any sense out of the latter half of this verse. Should we restore the expression to -khaiḥ śveta-, 
	 or nakhāśveta-?
{14} The bracketed letter which is left here may have been ga, as could be known from the reverse side of 
	 the impression, but it is not certain. The whole appears to be a name. The last letter too of this line
	 is damaged. 
{15} The reading of the bracketed letters is not certain, as they are damaged. THey were so read both by 
	 Acharya and Nahar; but grammatically the construction does not appear to be correct. 
{16} Drop tu for metrical exigencies. The correct form of the name is yaśodhara. The impression shows that
	 the letters up to this name in the line are now lost or damaged. They have been adopted here from 
	 Jinavijaya's transcript.
