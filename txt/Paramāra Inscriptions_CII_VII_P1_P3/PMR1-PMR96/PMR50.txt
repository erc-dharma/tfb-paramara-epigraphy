{1} From the rubbing, supplied to me through the courtesy of the Chief Epigraphist, at his request, by Prof.
	Van Name.
{2} In the fourth foot of v.7 the third letter is required to be guru, and the error can be eradicated by
	reading the letter as lla, as it was probably intended. Verse 8 has its first and fourth feet in the Indravajrā
	metre, whereas the second in the Vaṁśastha metre and the third in Indravaṁśā.
{3} Before this syllable there is an ornamental device which was taken by Kielhorn as a flower, but to me
	it appears to be a rayed Sun.
{4} The anusvāra on ra is surrounded by an ornamental device resembling a petal on each of the four sides.
{5} Here, as also in some other instances, the daṇḍas is engraved so close to the following letter as to appear as a mātrā.
{6} Read -vomvujāmvunagaukasaḥ, which would offend against the metre but which has to be corrected as śaṃ vobjāmvunagau-
	kasaḥ, to suit the metre. Parts of the third of these letters appear as scored off. This difficult verse was
	first explained by Hall as follows:
	"May Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva (Ka Ī Īśa)-in colour resembling, severally, the water lilly, the black
	bee, and the kāśa grass (kañjalka ali kāśa), having, respectively, for weapons, meaning utterances, a dis
	cus, and the pināka (huṁkāra ari pināka); moving, in order as enumerated, with birds, a bird, and a
	bull (vi vi gō), and whose abode is on the Jambū bearing mountain, bestow on you prosperity"(J.Am.O.
	Soc., Vol.VI, p.537). Kielhorn suggested to translate the last compound as 'whose abode is, respectively,
	a lotus, the water, and the mountain'(abja ambu naga). Kielhorn also read the first akṣara of the verse
	as kai, stating that the superscript line which turns ē into ai is very faint, but it is undoubttedly there.
	But he does not justify the reading Kaiśāh. And in this respect the following way of solving the com-
	pound is proposed; aśca īśaśca eśau; kaśca eśau ceti kaiśāḥ.
{7} As already noted by Kielhorn, one would expect -śate ṁke here.
{8} The sign of anusvāra was originally omitted and engraved subsequently, but it is only a dot, unlike others.
{9} This is following Kielhorn and the name appers in the other sources mentioned above. The con
	sonant of the akṣara in the impression before me is definitely la.
{10} This letter is badly engraved and also has a chisel mark above.
{11} The right hand curve of this letter appears to have been originally omitted and was engraved subsequently.
	 The daṇḍas at the beginning of this line are superfluous.
{12} Read vatsare. The first of these letters is damaged.
{13} A subsequent attempt was made to correct this akṣara.
{14} A curve resembling u - mātrā appears to have been attached to sa.
{15} Probably by a superfluous chisel mark the tri appears as vi.
{16} The daṇḍas are redundant and the first and the tenth letter of the first foot of this verse are damaged in
	the very process of engraving.
{17} This sign of visarga is evidently superflous.
{18} Read niketanaṃ. The following sa is so formed as to look like ṣa.
{19} The first two akṣaras of this word have each an anusvāra over it, and the last two akṣaras appear to
	 have been corrected subsequently. The consonant of ve is engraved as d.
{20} Kielhorn read puṣyatā, but the impression before me clearly shows the word as I have taken here.
{21} Kielhorn read maṇḍanaṃ, which I do not find in the impression before me.
{22} The superscript of this akṣara is engraved as m, as in some other cases also, which are not noted separately.
{23} A redundant superscript r was at first wrongly engraved on this letter and an attempt seems to have been 
	 made to score it off later.
{24} The daṇḍas are redundant. The first, third, fourth and the seventh akṣaras of this pāda are all wrongly
 	 engraved. As suggested by Kielhorn, read [taddāsaṃ] māṃ bhūta[le] suprasiddhaṃ.
{25} Read praśastiriyaṃ, but it would not suit the metre and hence I follow Kielhorn in reading praśaṃseyaṃ.
{26} The slanting stroke of the preceding akṣara is omitted and hence the letter appears as pa.
