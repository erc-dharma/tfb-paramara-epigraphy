PennGlobal: PMR96
Period: VS 1174 āṣāḍha śudi 5
Location: Jālōr
Dynasty: Paramāra
King: Vāsala
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Metrical in 5 verses.
Metres: Verse 1 and 3 Āryā; v.2 Śārdūlavikrīḍita; vv. 4-5 Anuṣṭubh
Gist: Records that Mēlaradēvi, queen of King Vīsala  of the Paramāra family, 
	  furnished the temple of Sindhurājēśvara with a golden cupola.
Published reference: Trivedi (1978), no. 96.

Text{1}

1. oṃ{2} | jetuṃ visvā(śvā)mitraṃ vaśiṣṭhamuninātikopa pūrṇṇena |
2. paramāraṇāya janita[ḥ] kuṃḍe tenaiva paramāra[ḥ] ||1||
3. āsīd vākpatirāja nāma nṛpatiḥ śrīpāramārā[nvaye]{3}
4. tatputrojani caṃdanā(no)vanipatiḥ{4} tannandano deva[rāṭ] |
5. tatputrastvaparājita[ḥ] samabhavat prauḍha pratāpa[ḥ  sudhīḥ]{5}
6. putrobhūd aparājitasya vijayī śrīvijjalo bhūpatiḥ{6} ||2||
7. senānīr iva{7} śaṃbhoḥ pradyumna ivātha vā hare[r nnūnaṃ(nam)?] |
8. dakṣo vāmvu(mbu)jasūte{8}r dhārāvarṣo narādhipatiḥ ||3||
9. dhārāvarṣasya putroyaṃ jāto vīsalabhūpatiḥ |
10. yena bhūmaṃḍalīkānāṃ dharmmamārggotra darśitaḥ ||4||
11. rājñī melaradevyā tu patnī{9} vīsalabhūpateḥ |
12. sauvarṇṇaṃ kalasaṃ mūrddhani siṃdhurājesva(śva)re kṛ{10}taṃ(tam) ||5||
13. [saṃ]vat 1174 āṣāḍha su(śu)di 5 bhaume ||{11}

--------------------------------------------------

{1} From ink-impressions.
{2} Expressed by a symbol.
{3} pa is changed to pā for the sake of the metre.
{4} The sandhi is not made here, as required by the rules.
{5} Reu: svayaṃ(yam).
{6} The visarga shows three dots here.
{7} After the third letter in this line there is a vacant space for two letters. The figures of speech in this 
	verse leave no doubts to conclude that Dhārāvarṣa was the son of Vijjala.
{8} Dakṣa was born of one who had himself his birth in a lotus, i.e., Brahmā.
{9} Probably some other letter was originally formed here. The words rājñī and patnī are to be changed 
	to the third case, to be connected with kṛtaṃ in the next line.
{10} The formation of the letter is rather peculiar.
{11} Some traces below show that at least one more line was there, originally, probably giving the name of 
	 the poet and the engraver.
