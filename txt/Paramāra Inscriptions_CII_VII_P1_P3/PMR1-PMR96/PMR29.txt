{1} From the facsimile (plate G) in An. Rep. of the Arch. Department of Nizam's Dominions (Hyderabad),
	for 1927-28, and revised from a fresh impression kindly supplied by Dr. R. Subrahmanyam, Superintend-
	ing Archaeologist, South Eastern Circle, now Professor in the Andhra University.
{2} Denoted by a symbol which is mutilated.
{3} Krishnamacharalu observed that "the sign which looks the secondary n below ma has to be omitted".
	This sign, however, which is clear in the Plate, has disappeared in the impression, leaving only a faint
	trace.
{4} The medial sign of vā is mutilated and a redundant anusvāra sign appears on the following akshara.
{5 }Krishnamacharlu reads -mātha-, which gives no sense. The horn above the left limb of the second of
	these letters is distinct, showing it to be dh; and the pṛṣṭha-mātrā of this letter has wrongly been
	taken by him as the medial ā attached to the preceding one.
{6} Viṣṇu became the arrow with which Śiva killed the demon Tripura. See Saura - Purāṇa, XXXV, 16 and
	also No. 60 v. 4, below. It is also interesting to note that verse 2 eulogises the creative aspect of the deity
	whereas the third verse emphasises his destructive aspect.
{7} Used for the dynastic name Paramāra which would not suit the metre.
{8} That is, the Sun and the moon.
{9} The consonant of the bracketed akṣara is formed as v.
{10} The akṣara in the brackets appears to have been corrected later on.
{11} Here Ganguly read dhyā and corrected it to ndhyā; but a trace above the horn shows that n was engraved;
	 and I agree with Krishnamacharlu in taking it as ndhyā. And in that case the dot above the preceding
	 akṣara has to be taken as a fault of the stone.
{12} This akṣara has a redundant curve above, making it appear as ti.
{13} Here the editor of Ep. Ind. remarks: "The Smṛtis enjoin that the study of vedas should be stopped  
	 when there is an evening thunder". cf. Yājñavalkya smṛti(T.S.S.), I, 144.
{14} The bracketed akṣara is so formed as also to appear as śro, as actually read by Krishnamacharlu.. But it
	 gives no sense.
{15} Ganguly translated this portion of the verse as this king "produced the only fitting lake which rivalled the 
	 ocean." But the word which was taken by him to mean 'produced' is utpannaḥ, as used here, and not
	 utpāditaḥ; and as far as I can see, the sense intended here is that the King's only suitable rival (or com
	 panion) is the ocean which collected the best gems from the universe (as he collected the best learned men).
	 It is arthopamā.
{16} The visarga is omitted according to the Vārtika on Pāṇinī, VIII,3,36.
{17} The reading of both these akṣaras is doubtful and a redundant anusvāra appears on the preceding khā,
	 if it is not a fault of the stone itself.
{18} This akṣara has not come out in the plate in the A.R. but it is clear in the impression.
{19} The akṣara in the brackets is mutilated in the Plate in the A.R. whereas in the impression with me it
	 is lost in the crack. Krishnamacharlu read it as so and Ganguly as sā; whereas the editor of the Ep.
	 Ind. remarked that it is schā, and in that case the visarga of the preceding letter is to be taken redundant.
	 I agree with Krishnamacharlu in taking the pada-cheda as saḥ arjunaḥ, the first of these words being
	 intended to denote the sense of 'well-known'.
{20} A sign or a letter was first engraved at the end of this line and was later on scratched.
{21} Probably some other akṣaras were originally incised in place of both these.
{22} The sign of visarga was subsequently inserted.
{23} The reading is uncertain. In his transcript Krishnamacharlu omitted seven letters after ha, and Ganguly
	 read them as given here. The intended reading appears to be vāhavyūheṣu heṣā, which also suits the metre.
{24} Originally trai, with the pṛṣṭha mātrā erased later on.
{25} Krishnamacharlu read: dvaṁdva dvaṁdva-, and Ganguly remarked that this expression does not yield
	 any plausible sense. Ganguly also read the preceding expression as pateUha and observed that between
	 te and ha there is no space for two akṣaras. The intended reading appears to be as given above; and
	 I also agree with the editor of the Ep.Ind. who takes it to mean "bowing to the couple(i.e., śiva and
	 Pārvatī) destroying the bad qualities (viz., passion and ignorance)". The word dvandva here means 'a
	 couple of opposite conditions or things', for which cf. śiśupāla vadha, IV.64.
{26} The reading of the bracketed akṣara is doubtful. It may also have been ja(for jya).
{27} These five akṣaras are mutilated and the reading is as suggested by the editor of Ep.Ind. The sign of
	 anusvāra above the second of them, which appears in the impression, is redundant.
{28} The bracketed letters are badly mutilated and the reading adopted here is only conjectural, so as to fill in
	 the gap.
{29} All these letters are lost and the reading suggested is: -mabhyucchritaśikharalasatpūrṇṇacandraṃ.
{30} The impression shows that some letters in the following line are totally lost, leaving traces, They may
	 have denoted the date.
