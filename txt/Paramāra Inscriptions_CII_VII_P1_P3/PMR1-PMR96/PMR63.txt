{1} From impressions.
{2} Denoted by the Nāgarī letter u, with anusvāra above.
{3} In the whole of this inscription the punctuation mark is very often used, sometimes even at the end of
	the first and the third foot of a verse and also to separate names. All these marks, when redundant,
	are here put in brackets, only to lissen the number of foot notes and corrections. Here read pradātāya
	(or pradātre ca) tasmai. The first foot of this verse offends against the metre, as in some other instances 
	also. The sign of visarga that precedes it is smaller and appears to have been inserted subsequently.
{4} Read - ṭairnāṃmabhiḥ. The first two letters which are now lost may have been sarvvaiṃ-. The visarga is
	not changed to r here.
{5} Read praśastirli-. The last two akṣaras of this verse may have been mayā or -'dhunā.
{6} Read dvyuttara-.
{7} Some other letter appears to have been originally engraved here.
{8} The intended reading may have been dalitārātivarggaḥ
{9} Read praśasti. With praśasti, the following expression is redundant.
{10} This visarga is changed to r in J. B. B. R. A. S., but sandhi is optional here. The daṇḍas in the 1. are 
	 redundant.
{11} Read śreyoyaśovṛddhaye. The following two letters which are missing may have been sandhi.
{12} Read śrīmatī. The daṇḍa after tāḥ appears to have been scored off.
{13} Gōṣṭhika means a member of the managing committee. See C. I. I., IV, p.21, n.3.
{14} This is a contraction of aṃśa. What appears as the sign of anusvāra above dra is a fault of the stone.
{15} Read kāśyaparṣi. From sutaḥ up to this letter the text is restored from J. B. B.R. A. S., as this part of the
	 stone is now broken and lost.
{16} Perhaps we have to read durggādityasutasametasya. The construction is not clear.
{17} J. B. B. R. A. S. has śrīdharaḥ, but I am certain about my reading of the name.
{18} The akṣara is now lost and has been restored from the reading in J. B. B. R. A. S.
{19} This and the preceding letter are now lost. They are restored from the reading in J. B. B. R. A. S.
{20} The second letter of the name may also be read as hu.
{21} J. B. B. R. A. S. has [dha]rmmarataḥ, but to me the intended reading seems to be [ṣaṭka]rmmarataḥ.
{22} The akṣara ha is engraved below the line.
{23} J. B. B. R. A. S. has -rmmalabuddhiḥ, which is evidently wrong.
{24} Read janmasu. Here the sense is not clear.
{25} Read iyaṃ, but it would not suit in the metre after the sandhi. From the beginning of this line up to ṣṭhā
	 the portion of the stone is lost and the reading is restored from J. B. B. R. A. S.
{26} Read kṣantavyaṃ. The following five akṣaras cannot be connected with this.
{27} These four akṣaras are again restored from J. B. B. R. A. S.
{28} The intended sense appears to be that 'as the well has been constructed with dressed stones, there should
	 be no anxiety about break in it'. tāti may have been wrongly engraved for tābhiḥ which again is
	 to be corrected to tasmāt.
