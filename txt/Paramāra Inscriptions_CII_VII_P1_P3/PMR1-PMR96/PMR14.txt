Period: VS 1091 
Location: British Museum 
Dynasty: Paramāra
King: Bhojadeva
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Prose and  Metrical in a verse
Metre: Śārdūlavikrīḍita
Gist: Dedicatory verse incised on the pedestal of an image of Vāgdevī(Sarasvatī)
Published reference: Trivedi (1978), no. 14. 

Text{1}

1. oṃ(om) || srau{2}(śrī)mad bhoja nareṃdra caṃdra nagarī vidyādharī -U{3} dhīḥ yo[sā ?] nāma U-[sasma*]{4} khalu sukhaṃ prasthāpyatāṃ
2. yā[psa]rāḥ | vāgdevī[ṃ] prathama[ṃ]{5} vidhāya jananīṃ yasyārjjitānāṃ trayī - - - U phalādhikāṃ vara(ruciṃ ?) mūrttiṃ su[śu]bhāṃ ni-{6}
3. rmmame || iti subhaṃ(śubham) || sūtradhārasahirasutamaṇathaleṇa(na){7} ghaṭitaṃ(tam) || vi. ni.{8} si(śi)vadevena likhitamiti ||
4. saṃvat 1091 ||

-----------------------------------------------------------

{1} From a photograph. It also shows two letters in the corner just beside the proper right top, both of the 
	same time as the inscription.
{2} The subscript r of this akṣara has not come out on the photograph and it is possible that if at all
	engraved, it was not deep enough.
{3} Both these akṣaras are mutilated, but as there is space for one only, it appears that they may have been
	crisped into each other. Conjecturally to be restored as saṃsu(संसु). Also, perhaps nareṃdranaṃdanakarī(नरेंन्द्रनंदनकरी) was intended.
{4} Mutilated. Adopted from Dikshit's reading. Saukhyāyāmarasaṃsadaḥ(?).
{5} This and the preceding sign of anusvāra, which have not come out on the photograph, are perhaps clear on
	the original.
{6} These three akṣaras have been restored from the traces left; and the reading is uncertain.
{7} At both places in the name nasal is lingual and not dental as taken by Dikṣit.
{8} The forst of the letters in this word is mutilated and the second is illegible. Perhaps what was intetded here 
	is vijñānika, as shown by the traces. The reading of ni is not certain.
