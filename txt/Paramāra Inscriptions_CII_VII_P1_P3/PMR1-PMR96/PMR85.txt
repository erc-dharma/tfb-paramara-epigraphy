{1} From the original stone and an ink impression. 
{2} Some other letter was first engraved here.
{3} This letter and the third following it, which were originally omitted, were subsequently written at the 
	top, with a cross-mark to show their exact position. 
{4} The visarga after ya is omitted here in accordance with the Vārttika on Pāṇini, VIII, 3, 36. 
{5} The reading of the first two syllables in this line is conjectural and has been suggested in view of the 
	following ra. Both the syllables are lost. 
{6} Here is a kāka pada sign to show that the verse continues in the next line. 
{7} The reading of the bracketed letters is only conjectural, so as to suit the metrical exigencies. 
{8} The reading of this letter is doubtful. On the stone the letters are clear as tritayaṃ nānyasaṃgatā, but they give 
	no meaning, and it is also prosodically faulty. 
{9} Here is a pun on the word sūra, meaning the sun, and śūra, i.e., a heroic person. 
{10} Here eight syllables are lost, of which the last appears to be rā. 
{11} Nine syllables are lost here. 
{12} Here eleven syllables are lost, the first of which have left some traces and the last one shows the 
	 mātrā of a long ī. Perhaps the intended reading is nadī-, or sarit-.
{13} Excepting the first two and the last two letters, the whole of the first half of this verse is lost. 
{14} Eleven syllables are lost here. 
{15} Almost the whole of the latter half of this verse is lost. 
{16} The letters are clear in the impression but the meaning is unintelligible to me. It may be the name, or 
	 we may read gehinī(?). 
{17} The whole of the fourth foot and the number of this verse are lost. 
{18} The fourth foot of verse 17 along with the numerical figure denoting it, and the first foot of the next 
	 verse are lost here. 
{19} Thirteen syllables of the beginning of this line and eleven of the first foot and two of the second foot 
	 of verse 19 are lost here. The mātrā on ṇa appears to have been inserted subsequently. The verse is 
	 intended to say that Bhavyāsarāja, the younger brother of Āsadēva, was appointed as a customs house officer. 
	 For prasāra in this sense, see the śiśupāla vadha, II, 64, where Mallinātha quotes Vaijayantī:
	 'prasārastṛṇakāṣṭhādeḥ praveśaḥ', vahikā karṇa is the office of the village or town accountant. See E. C. D., p.206.
{20} The whole of the fourth foot along with the number of this verse is lost. 
{21} A kāka pada sign appears here. The following two letters may have been dakṣaḥ.
{22} The second and third feet of this verse are totally lost. The reading of the letter that follows is only 
	 conjectural but the curve of the mātrā above is partly visible. 
{23} Of this letter there are only traces, and it appears to have been followed by yā, at the beginning of the 
	 next line. 
{24} Almost the second and the third pādas of this verse are completely lost. 
{25} The bracketed letters are restored from the traces left. 
{26} The reading of this letter is conjectural. It may have been followed by kara in the next line. 
{27} The rest of v. 25 is all lost and the following bracketed letters are restored from the traces left. 
{28} About a dozen letters are visible in the form of traces but none completely. 
{29} Almost the whole line has peeled off. 
{30} Nothing more than given here is intelligible in this and the following line. 
{31} This is a single word between two verses(32 and 33) and is probably intended to show a dual of them. 
{32} Here the record ends with a floral design.
