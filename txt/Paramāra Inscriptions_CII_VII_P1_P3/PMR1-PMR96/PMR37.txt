{1} From the impression and the original stone.
{2} Expecting these three, all the akṣaras in this line, which contained two and a quarter verses, are lost.
	Some are visible in the form of traces.
{3} As here, at the beginning of each line also, the missing akṣaras are supplied by context, of course con-
	jecturally, and are put in brackets.
{4} The akṣara in brackets is damaged; but it is more like ta than ca.
{5} This akṣara, as it can be read on the stone, is de and not ṭe, as taken by Sircar.
{6} The sign of visarga was inserted subsequently.
{7} The medial ā attached to this letter was subsequently scored off as unnecessary.
{8} The anusvāra appears on the left side of the curve.
{9} What appear like anusvāra signs on this and the preceding three letters are only scratches on the stone.
{10} The latter half of this verse probably means to say that when the goddedd of the sky is full of anxiety
	 (because of the thought of separation from you at the time of the evening) you take the blame on your
	 own self (svayamāttaḥ doṣasya āveśaḥ yena).
{11} The word kara here means 'hand' and 'ray'; and the word tārakāḥ means both 'the stars' and 'the
	 pupils of eyes'. The figures of speech is śleṣa.
{12} It appears that first ḍaṃ was engraved and subsequently it was corrected to ṭaṃ.
{13} A floral design is engraved here between double daṇḍas. It is to show that the eulogy ends here.
{14} Similarly, another floral design appears here between double daṇḍas.
{15} Unfortunately, the name of the person who wrote the composition on the stone before it was engraved,
	 is lost. It appears to have ended with the second or third letter of 1.12.
{16} This letter is usually found at the end of some inscriptions, denoting the end or auspiciousness. See Ep.
	 Ind., Vol. XXX, p.218, n. 1. The double daṇḍas on either of its sides show that the record is complete.
