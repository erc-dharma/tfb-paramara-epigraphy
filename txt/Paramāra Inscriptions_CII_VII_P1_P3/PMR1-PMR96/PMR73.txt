Text{1}

1. oṃ{2} || śrī varddhamāna vibhuradbhuta śāradeṃdur doṣānuṣaṃma(ga) vimukhaḥ subhagaḥ śubhābhiḥ | āḍhyaṃ bhaviṣṇuramalābhirasau kalābhiḥ saṃtāpamarttayatu
   kaumudasā(mā)tanotu ||1 śrīmati dhārāvarṣe vikramatarṣe pramāra kula harṣe | aṣṭādaśaśata deśottaṃse caṃdrāvatīharṣe ||2 śrīmat kelha-
2. ṇa maṃḍala pati tanayāyāṃ nayaika śālinyāṃ(nyām) | tat paṭṭa praṇayinyāṃ śṛṃgārapadopapada devyāṃ(vyām) ||3 etad grāma prābhava vaibhava bhṛti tat pradatta sācivye |
   sakala kalā kuśale gṛhamedhini nāga[ṭe]{3} sacive ||4 [dviḥ]smaraśaradinakaramitavarṣe śuciśasyasaṃpadutkarṣe | [duṃ]dubhināmani{4} dhāmani
3. viṭapa pallavita dharmya dhiyāṃ(yām) ||5 etat ṣaṭka catuṣkikā viracita śrī [maṃ]ḍapoddhārataḥ puṇyaṃ paṇya[magaṇya]mākalayati śrīvīragoṣṭhī janaḥ |
   manye kiṃ tu(nu?) catuṣkikā dvayamidaṃ dattā(ttvā ?)bhimukhyasthiti stheyas tat kalimoha bhūpayugalīṃ jitvātapatra dvayīṃ(yīm){5} ||6 iṃduḥ kuṃda sitaiḥ-

4. [śalakṣmīṃ mu]hu{6}r yāvad bhānurasau tanoti paritopyāśāḥ prakāso(śo)jva(jjva)lāḥ | tāvaddhārmika dharmakarma{7} rabhasa prāravdha(bdha) kalyāṇika sto-
   trādy ucchava gīta vādyavidhibhiḥ jīvettrikaṃ sarvataḥ ||7 rājñā śṛṃgāradevyātra vāṭikābhūmiradbhutā | dattā śrīvīra pūjārthaṃ śāsva(śva)taḥ [śreyasaḥ]{8}

   prabhasūriṇāṃ kṛtiriyaṃ(yam) ||cha||saṃvat 1255 āsoya(ja) sudi 7 vu(bu)dhavāre sakalagoṣṭhikalokaḥ trikoddhāraṃ svaśreyase kārita

{1} From impressions.
{2} Expressed by a symbol which is partly visible.
{3} This akṣara is partly lost and the reading of Muni Jinavijaya is adopted here.
{4} From an examination of the original, the akṣara in brackets appears more like uṃ and the reading of the 
	first two akṣaras in the next line is also doubtful, as the consonant of the first of them is damaged
	and the second looks like a conjunct. The verse offends against the metre Āryā since it has only fourteen
	and not fifteen mātrās, as required. 
{5} catuṣka means a 'hall resting on four pillars or a quadrangular cour-yard'; and ṣaṭka is used here
	to denote six figures of Tīrthaṅkaras. The intended sense appears to be that the goṣṭhikas(members
	of the assembly) constructed a maṇḍapa for the six figures, and besides these, two (or two of the same?)
	were constructed by one whose name is not mentioned here but who is stated to have conquered kali 
	and moha thereby 
{6} This with the preceding akṣara is indistinct and the reading is conjectural as from traces left.
{7} It cannot be ascertained whether the consonant m is doubled in all the three cases, as some others also.
{8} The reading of the bracketed akṣaras is only from the traces left and it may also have been svayaśaḥ, or 
	śreyse, which suits the sense. 
{9} This akṣara has now totally disappeared and the reading is from that of the Muni, referred to above.
