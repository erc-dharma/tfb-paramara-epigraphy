{1} From a photograph.
{2} Expressed by a symbol.
{3} Sandhi is not performed here.
{4} The horizontal middle bar of s(स्) in sta is not clearly visible.
{5} This sign of punctuation is superfluous. Moreover, here, and in some cases below which are not pointed
	out separately, the rules of sandhi have not been observed.
{6} Colebrook read rājaśāsana; and the correction is due to Kielhorn
{7} Kielhorn read the second letter of the name as rā, putting in brackets. But the photograph with me
	clearly shows that the consonant is v(व्) and not r(र्).
{8} The daṇḍa is superfluous and the insertion of tathā with the dual in grāmayoḥ is grammatically wrong.
{9} The reading of the consonant put in brackets is doubtful, as observed by Kielhorn also.
{10} The daṇḍa is superfluous.
{11} The first two letters of the name are completely blurred in the photograph.
{12} This and the preceding seven letters have not clearly come out in the photograph, and on the plate too
	 they appear to be so indistinct that Kielhorn had to observe that "Coolbrooke read these very indistinct
	 consonants as dy; and similarly, Mr. Wilkinson, in the Journ. Bengal. As. Soc., Vol. VII,p.737, 1.15, has
	 samit kuśa til ānn ādy āhutibhir. Mr. Fleet, ante, Vol.XVI, p.254, 1.14 read til ānnāṣṭāhutibhir." The
	 initial protion of the following four lines too is equally blurred but it can all be made out from the
	 context.
{13} As we find in some other grants of the house, this verse is put by way of a parenthesis, and the sentence is
	 continued with evamākalayya in 1.11.
{14} Colebrooke has adrelavaddhāvarī, and Kielhorn read it a[dra]le[vi?]ddhāvarī. But the second akṣara has a faint curve
	 above to show that it may be dri; and the mātrā of the fourth letter is equally faint. But it is short
	 (and not long, as Kielhorn took it).
{15} Kielhorn suggested that the last letter, ya, should be read as yā, observing sandhi with the following ā.
	 But it is not necessary as the sandhi is not compulsory here, according to the observation vākye'tha sā vivakṣaa-
	 mapekṣate. On the other hand, the fourth letter, i.e. ja, combined with the following ā, is to be read as jā,
	 and the sign of visarga after tri has to be dropped.
{16} Kielhorn read here dakṣiṇā[yāta?]. But a faint trace of the medial ā after the consonant d(द्) and the traces of
	 the last two syllables are there to confirm my reading.
{17} Read avasathika, as suggested by COlebrooke and adopted by Kielhorn.
{18} Read grāmothavaṇakagrāmau or better, vaḍaudothavaṇakagrāmau.
{19} Read paradattāṃ vā.
