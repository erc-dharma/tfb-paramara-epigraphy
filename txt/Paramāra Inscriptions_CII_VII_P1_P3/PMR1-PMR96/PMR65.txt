{1} From an impression, subsequently also comparing the reading from the original.
{2} Expressed by a symbol.
{3} Both these akṣaras are damaged but the consonant of the first of them is definitely s. The reading
	is as may be expected here.
{4} The first letter of the name of the month has not distinctly come out in the impression and the reading
	has been adopted from the Annual Rep. on Ind. Ep., No. B-718 of 1961-62, where the date has been
	calculated.
{5} Read amāvāsyām.
{6} Probably acalapure is intended.
{7} Read caṃdrāvatyām. These two letters and some of those in the preceding line are misformed but the read-
	ing appears to be certain.
{8} Here the sibilant, as occasionally elsewhere, appears as a combination of the palatal and the dental.
{9} The pṛṣṭha mātrā of this akṣara is engraved as the sign of the secondary i and is attached to the pre-
	ceding letter.
{10} The consonant following dha is so carelessly formed as to make one indefinite about its reading; but
	 the mātrā of ā attached to it is certain.
{11} The sign of visarga was inserted subsequently, though it is certain.
{12} The writing here too is very careless and one would be tempted to read the name Angadatta, but it
	 would not be constructed and the sense would in that case remain incomplete.
{13} The meaning of the six syllables ending with this letter is not clear.
{14} The daṇḍa is redundant.
{15} The rest of the akṣaras in this line and almost all in the following line are lostusual ass imprecation
	 appears to have been engraved here.
