{1} From the original and an impression.
{2} Metre: Sragdharā
{3} It is not known if this akṣara was to(तो), for a vertical after it, if engraved at all, is lost. At first,
	tuṃmadhutā was engraved, as later on, the sign of anusvāra was erased, retaining the mark, and the consonant
	of the last was changed to n(न).
{4} Metre in this and in the following three lines: śārdūlavikrīḍita.
{5} Because of a redundant stroke, this letter appears as sva.
{6} Read -uddhataṃ, or samuddhataṃ. Probably nākaujasāṃ = 'of the gods' is intended. Va changed to vā, metris causa.
	The first letter in paścime was originally cut as pā and later on the sign of the mātrā was scored off as
	redundant.
{7} Metre in this and the following line: Sragdharā. Read thaḥ. The word Paramāra is changed to Paramāra,
	for metre.
{8} These akṣaras are partly visible but they cannot be made out. The last two in this line are restored
	conjecturally.
{9} Metre in this and the following two lines: Śārdūlavikrīḍita. The reading of the bracketed letter that
	follows is certain, but it gives no sense.
{10} The unit figure of the number is lost. Also note the invented origin of the word Kāyastha.
{11} The bracketed letter is erased (?). The phrase gives no sense.
{12} Metre in II.11-13: Sragdharā.
{13} The sign of anusvāra is erased and the consonant of the third letter is changed to t. SHould we read
	 tatiratulāḥ, which also suits the metre (?)
{14} Metre in this and the next two lines: Śārdūlavikrīḍita.
{15} This is apparently a local deity. The name appears to be connected with Vajrata. Occurring in No.24.
	v. II. But also see n. on this name, in the edition of the inscription, above, on p.196.
{16} Metre: Anuṣṭubh. The lacunae at the end of this line may be filled in by reading samaprabhaḥ.
{17} Metre in this line and in the beginning of the next: Śārdūlavikrīḍita. The reading of the first four
	 akṣaras in this line, which are indistinct, is not certain.
{18} Metre: Sragdharā. This is evidently the description of Bhoja.
{19} Metre in this and the next two lines: Śārdūlavikrīḍita.
{20} The unit figure of the number is lost.
{21} That is, Sahasrārjuna who ruled Māhiṣmatī and was killed by Paraśurāma.
{22} The consonant of this akṣara is formed as s. Metre in this and the next line: Sragdharā.
{23} Metre of verse in this and the next line: Śārdūlavikrīḍita.
{24} Probably -vaṃśajā, but the rest of the line is lost. The next line contains parts of only two akṣaras
	 , which cannot be made out.
{25} Metre: Śārdūlavikrīḍita.
{26} Metre: Sragdharā, Kāndiśīka means frightened, or 'afraid'. Literally, one who has lost the direction.
{27} The latter part of this akṣara is lost.
{28} The letters in the brackets are altogether lost and are conjecturally supplied, as the last one also in this line.
	 No sandhi is made between śrī and the following ē, of which we have some other instances below. In the
	 beginning of this line the lost letters appear to be mālava-.
{29} The reading is certain but no intelligible sense can be made out.
{30} Metre: Śārdūlavikrīḍita. The first letter which is lost may have been ko(को).The number of the verse is
	 lost.
{31} These two letters are slightly damaged but the reading is certain. SOme of the following punctuation
	 marks are redundant.
{32} The bracketed letters in this line are all lost and their restoration is conjectural but almost certain.
{33} The consonant of the first of these akṣaras looks (or, is) somewhat as d and the second has left only
	 traces. It may also have been ṇ.
{34} Read savṛkṣa-.
{35} Read -kapūrvvakaṃ.
{36} Sandhi is not made here, as also in some other instances below. The last two akṣaras in the line are
	 obviously -vena.
{37} This letter stands for Paṃḍita. It is noteworthy that this is used with the name of Kāyastha, who was
	 also a trader.
{38} Should we read maṇḍapikādāyāt, i.e., from the house where the articles brought for sale were taxed?
{39} This word is used without any case suffix. The last five letters in the line have to be restored as 
	 tad=ādāyānudinaṃ.
{40} The first two akṣaras in the line appear to have been pāla.
{41} The sign of visarga appears to have been later on inserted. Maṇivāya is rather an uncommon word and
	 may denote a 'jeweller who prepares strings of precious beads', on the analogy of tantuvāya from tantu. It
	 appears that they are shown by the number 4, without giving their names.
{42} It is not known whether Rāūtā or Mahirāūtā was the name.
{43} Probably cha(छ), denoting the end of the record.
