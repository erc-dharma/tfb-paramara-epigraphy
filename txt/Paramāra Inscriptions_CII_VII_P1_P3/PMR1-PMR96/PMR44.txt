{1} From facsimiles in Ep. Ind., Vol. XXIV, facing p.232.
{2} Expressed by a symbol.
{3} A mark at the end of the line, as of some others not noted below, shows that the word is continued in the
	next line.
{4} Some other letter, probably nu, was originally engraved here.
{5} My reading shows that the line ends in a ś(श्) which is very faintly visible, and accordingly, we have the 
	name Hariśchandra here.
{6} The name of the village can also be read a dodarapadra-. It appears that originally a vertical was put after
	the preceding ddha and later on it was scratched off only in the middle and thus we have the two dots
	showing a visarga.
{7} The daṇḍas are redundant.
{8} Chakravarti remarked that the omission of visarga after ra(र) is according to the vārttika: "kharpare śari vā
	visarga lopo vaktavyaḥ". But to me it seems equally probable that the word pura is used to end the name
	of the place and thus sthitena is left separate. Thus the expression may be taken also in the sense of
	'while residing or encamped at'.
{9} Originally, the letter was engraved as ki, and later on the curve of the mātrā appears to have been mixed
	with the preceding repha, and the vertical scored off by two small strokes faintly visible.
{10} A redundant slanting stroke makes the pa appear as ṣa.
{11} Chakravarti corrected it to saṃsāra[syā*]sārataṃ.
{12} Chakravarti read dharmaḥ, but I find the double mma, in the facsimile.
{13} Here and in some cases below the sign of anusvāra on da is faintly visible.
{14} Here and below, we need not change the ṭhi to ṭhī, as done by Chakravarti, as it is a compound word
	 requiring the original form of the first word.
{15} This mark of punctuation is redundant.
{16} This is a contraction of paṇḍita.
{17} The second letter of the name more likely appears to be lhū. 
{18} As Chakravarti has also noted, seven or eight letters specifying the gotra are completely effaced at the 
	 end of the line. Moreover, it is possible that the first syllable of the father's name may also have been
	 vā, as the left limb thereof is not horned and occasionally, though rarely, we find the verticals of vā also
	 joined by a horizontal stroke.
{19} Seven or eight letters are completely effaced. Moreover, both the letters of the first name are also effaced.
	 Chakravarti read the first name appearing in this line as sotā.
{20} As n.3 on the preceding page.
{21} The reading of the second letter of the name is not certain.
{22} The punctuation mark is redundant, as also in some of the lines below.
{23} As suggested by Chakravarti, read brāhmaṇānāmekonaviṃśateḥ.
{24} The dictionary meaning of tala is 'lowness, a forest, and a pound'. What is exactly meant here is not 
	 known. Chakravarti takes the word in the sense of 'a suburb', but this is not found in the dictionary.
{25} The punctuation mark is redundant. SOme of the lines at the end, and also where they are broken by
	 the Garuḍa figure, are marked by daṇḍa, which is not noted here in every instance. This tendency is
	 found more developed in Udayavarman's grant, below (No.46).
{26} As n.10 on the last page.
{27} The word vrā(brā)hmaṇa is superfluous here.
{28} As Chakravarti has noted "the left hand stroke of the medial ō in nō is joined to the preceding ha".
{29} This line is engraved in bigger letters, almost in the middle, and śrīḥ at the end.
