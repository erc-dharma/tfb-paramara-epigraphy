{1} From a set of photographs and the facsimile referred to above.
{2} Expressed by a symbol. The curve following the symbol is the punctuation mark.
{3} The subscript of this letter is curved as va(व).
{4} The vertical of this letter is not carved here and also in some cases below.
{5} Read pādānudhyāta. The first two lettters that follow this expression are damaged.
{6} THis letter was originally omitted and later on engraved below the line where it is to be supplied.
{7} As noted above, it appears to be an error for bhoktṛ(भोक्तृ).
{8} D.C.S.: dorddasya(दोर्द्दस्य). But to me it appears that what he takes to be the superscript r(र्) is a part of the 
	letter just above. Shastri also reads it as deddasya(देद्दस्य). MOreover, as indicated above, the sixth case is here
	used for the fourth, according to the rule sambandhasāmānye ṣaṣṭhī (सम्बन्धसामान्ये षष्ठी) (?). Chāturjātaka=a high local officer. See
	Ep.Ind., Vol.I.p.278.
{9} D.C.S: kantikā(kaṇikā)(कन्तिका(कणिका)). But the subscript of the second of these letters is clearly ch(छ्). The word
	kacchikā (कच्छिका) is a diminutive form of kacch (कच्छ) meaning 'margin or boundary region.' The word bhūmī(भूमी) is wrongly
	used, here and below, with long medial ī. There is a redundant punctuation mark following each word here
	and also in II. 16-19 below.
{10} What appears to be intended is saṃyuktā(संयुक्ता).
{11} Sircar remarks that upānasya(उपानस्य) is a mistake for oupamanyava(औपमन्यव).
{12} Probably the fourth case ending is intended here.
{13} D.C.S.:kāreṇa (क(का)रेण). But the fourth letter is clearly चा, with the verticals joined in the middle, as in the 
	 case of dhā(धा). Shastri also read the letter as I do.
{14} Read pārśvikā likhyante पार्श्विका लिख्यन्ते. These persons were all known to the royal preceptor. Shastri restored the first of
	 these letters to parīkṣitā (परीक्षिता).
{15} D.C.S. took two names here. tāta and nāṭa. Whereas Shastri only one name and read tātanāya. To me it 
	 appears to be one name and the last akṣara in the line is so formed as to be read either ya or ṭa followed 
	 by a punctuation mark. Similarly, the first letter in the following name may be read either pa, as the photo 
	 shows, or as va, as in the facsimile.
{16} Restore goggakādīnāṃ pārśvakatve (गोग्गकादीनां पार्श्वकत्वे). Shastri restored the second of these words to parīkṣya(परीक्ष्य). In these two lines each 
	 name is unnecessarily separated by a daṇḍa.
{17} All the three letters are damaged and probably can also be read as datritā(दत्रिता) for (दत्तेति).
{18} D.C.S.: Better read yathā(यथा).
{19} It is difficult to say whether the intended name is vaivasvata वैवस्वत. Sircar observed that the intended reading may 
	 also be vaivasuta (वैवसुत). But the whole can also be read as tathā ca ivaśu[ḥ]. The following letter gu is equally doubtful 
	 and it is not known if gṛhapati is intended.
{20} Reading uncertain.
{21} Read samutkīrṇaṃ
{22} Read caitat lekhakena. It may be remarked here that the letter ca shows a pṛṣṭha mātrā on the photograph
	 but not in the facsimile.
{23} The reading of the syllable in the brackets is not certain.
{24} There are two spiral symbols between the double daṇḍas.
