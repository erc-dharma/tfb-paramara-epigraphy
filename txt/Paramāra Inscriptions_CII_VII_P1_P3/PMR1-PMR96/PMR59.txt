Text{1}

1. siddham{2} | saṃ 1323 varṣe vaisāṣa(śākha) śu 7 vuddha(budha)dine
2. [puṣya]nakṣatre{3} adya(dye)ha vaḍovyapatta[ne] sama[sta] rājā-
3. valī sahita jaisiṃghadevarājye{4} tasminkā-
4. le varttamāne gauḍānvaye paṃthidode[su]ta-
5. praṃ(paṃ)mahaṇasīṃ(siṃ)hasuta-paṃ-raṇasīṃ(siṃ)hena{5}
6. vāṭikāudyāpanārthe{6} kīrttisthalo-
7. yaṃ(yam){7} | [su(śu)]bhaṃ bhavatu -{8} śrī[ḥ*] ||

-----------------------------------------------------------------

{1} From an inked impression.
{2} Expressed by a symbol.
{3} The first two letters in this line, as some others below, are not well formed; but the reading is certain.
{4} Read jayasiṃha-.
{5} The second akṣara in this word is probably to be corrected to śrī. In the Annual Report on Epigraphy,
	referred to above, it is taken as given here and it is also stated in it that Raṇasiṃha, whose name ends in
	siṃha, may not have been a Brāhmaṇa and therefore the prefix paṃ, that is, Paṇḍita, cannot be applied
	to him. But names with the suffix siṃha are also found among the Bhrāhmaṇas, for example, that of
	Jaitrasiṃha, the father of the Paramāra Arjunavarman's royal preceptor Govinda, for which see No.49.
{6} Read vāṭikodyāpanārthaṃ.
{7} Read -sthalamidaṃ. Ramsharma read kīrtti[ḥ*] sthā[pi*][te]yaṃ.
{8} Here, the impression shows a sign resembling the Nāgarī figure 5; and it is not known if it is a mātrā
	combined with the daṇḍa that follows, which is in fact needed here. Ramsharma took it the Nāgarī 5
	and explained it as "repeated five times." But it may perhaps be the sign of a misformed double daṇḍa.
