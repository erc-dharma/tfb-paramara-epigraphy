PennGlobal: PMR41
Period: Undated
Location: Bhopal
Dynasty: Paramāra
King: Lakṣmīvarman
Language: Sanskrit
Script: Nagari
Text: Prose
Gist: Records that feudatory of Lakṣmīvarman, s/o Yaśovarman, brother  of Jayavamran, 
	  gained victory over an enemy in battle.
Published reference: Trivedi (1978), no. 41.


Text{1}

First Side
 
1. om{2} svasti || śrīrjayo abhyudayaśca{3} || śrīparama[ā]-
2. rānvaye samastaparikriyāvirājamāna ||{4}
3. mahākumāraśrīla[smī(kṣmī)]varmmadevapra[khā ?]tā{5}
4. [śrīadhi ?]droṇācāryānvaye mahārājapu-
5. traśrīajayapāladeva[putra]mahārāya(ja)putra -

Second Side

6. śrīpīthanadevastatputramahārājaputraśrī-
7. tejovarmmadevasa(s)tatkaniṣṭhabhrātṛ[vya]{6} -
8. śrīvijayasiṃhena saṃhatya ca saṃdatya{7} ||
9. rāṣṭrakūṭānvaye rājaputraśrīvaddige-
10. na saha saṃjātayuddhesva(ṣva)rivijayaṃ(yaḥ) kra(kṛ) -

Third Side

11. tamiti(ta iti ||) kṛtiriyaṃ [rā]maputra[śrī]vija-
12. yasiṃhasya || tadanvayaḥ rā{8} (?) || pālheka[rā]-
13. vālheka rā | - ta - - - | - - - - - ||
14. ka - dhalā vā - - - - maṃḍale{9} rālāgrāma-
15. ḥ - kkāṃ pra[kṣi]pyata sa tena prāpta[ḥ*] ||{10}


------------------------------------------------------

{1} From impressions.
{2} Expressed by a symbol.
{3} The sign of the superscript on ja is faintly visible. The whole expression to be amended to -jayobhyu-.
{4} The sign of punctuation is superfluous; and the word has to be amended to prakriyā, meaning 'the bearing
	of royal insignia'.
{5} The last but one letter in this line is not well engraved and also mutilated. It does not give any intelligible
	sense. Possibly the whole word has to be read as prakhyātaḥ as suggested by Katare, or pra[sā]dāt.
{6} This is as read by Katare. It is also possible that the whole word may have been bhrātṛja, as traces
	indicate. The letter ta before kaniṣṭha is engraved as tka.
{7} The punctuation mark is superfluous. Moreover, the intended sense is unintelligible to me. Katare reads
	saṃva(ba)dhya in the sense of matrimonially connected, but the second letter is clearly da and not va, and
	the use of ca between the two words to be connected presents a difficulty here if we agree with him.
{8} This appears to be a contraction of rāṇaka.
{9} The akṣaras giving the name of the maṇḍala are mutilated beyond recognition.
{10} As stated above, the fragmentary state of the inscription does not enable us to have any sense out of the
	 last four lines. It is also surprising that the poet's name is mentioned before this portion and not in the 
	 end, as is usually the case.
