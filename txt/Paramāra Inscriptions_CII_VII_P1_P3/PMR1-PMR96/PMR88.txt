Gist: During the reign of Cāmuṇḍarājā a temple in honour of Śiva  was built by Hari,
	  one of the six sons of a goldsmith named Dōhaḍa, a resident of the fort of Citrakūṭa in Mēdapāṭa. 
Published reference: Trivedi (1978), no. 88.

{1} From an impression and personal examination of the original stone. 
{2} Expressed by a symbol which is partly visible. 
{3} Probably the reading is vyāptatamopahaḥ. A number of the akṣaras in this inscription are mutilated, and some 
	others are misformed, and these errors are not always pointed out for the fear of increasing the footnotes.
	Redundant and missing strokes also are not every time drawn attention to for the same reason, e.g., the 
	consonant of p in parama- is engraved as ṣa and vice versa is the case of the consonant ṣa in doṣa in the 
	preceding line. 
{4} The first word here also appears as puṣyat. Above ta of bhūṣita a redundant repha was at first 
	engraved and then scored off. The sandhi is not formed here as also in some places below, e.g., in 
	v.6. The reading of the bracketed letters that follow is from traces left. 
{5} This punctuation mark, as also some others below, is redundant. This too is not every time drawn 
	attention to. 
{6} Excepting a slight change in the third quarter, this verse is a copy of v.7 of No.85. 
{7} These four letters are mutilated and neither the reading is certain nor is the meaning clear. 
{8} The first akṣara of the name is mutilated and the third is overwritten, and thus the reading of both 
	these is not certain. 
{9} Originally su, with the mātrā perhaps erased later on. 
{10} The visarga is not dropped here. 
{11} This akṣara may have been tva.
{12} A number of the akṣaras of this verse cannot be made out but the general sense is to compare this 
     person with Viṣṇu by using such expressions as may be applicable to both. The reading may be śārṅgapāṇe-.
{13} What appears as the sign of anusvāra above this letter is either a redundant stroke or an abrasion. 
{14} Originally ta, later on changed. 
{15} Reading uncertain. 
{16} Here, the inscription shows traces of three and not four akṣaras as required by the metre. 
{17} Both these letters, which are mutilated beyond recognition, may have been naraiḥ. 
{18} A word denoting temple may have been employed here, but perhaps prāsāda was written. In his notice 
	 of the inscription in the Report of the Western Circle, 1908-09, p.49, D.R. Bhandarkar took the first 
	 two akṣaras of this stich as ārā, instead of atro as we read, and took the name as Ārāthūṇaka, con-
	 necting it with Ārthūṇaka, i.e., Arthūṇā. But to me the reading seems to be as taken here. 
{19} The sign of visarga is lightly scratched off and that of anusvāra is put here. The word means the sky. 
{20} The reading of these three akṣaras is doubtful and the first of them is written in the level of the margin 
	 on the proper right side of the stone, with an arrow mark where it is to be supplied. 
{21} The twelfth akṣara of the fourth foot of this verse is not long as metrically required. The lacuna may 
	 be supplied with tāṃgama. 
{22} Probably susandhānā may have been intended here. 
{23} This akṣara is mutilated and the preceding one is altogether indistinct. 
{24} The intended akṣaras were probably viṃva.
{25} Two akṣaras are lost here. Could they be jana? 
{26} The whole verse 25 is lost, and of the one that follows, only six letters are preserved. 
{27} The next two akṣaras which are lost may conjecturally be restored as kṣitā. 
{28} Almost all the letters in this line are indistinct. 
{29} From here the impression fails to give any intelligible and connected reading.
{30} The rest of the line is altogether indistinct and the letters in the next line are only partly preserved. The
