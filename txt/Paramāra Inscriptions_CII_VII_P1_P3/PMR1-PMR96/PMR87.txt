{1} From the original stone and inked impressions. Besides the one supplied by the Curator of the Rāj-
	putānā Museum, Ajmer, I had another impression from the Curator of the Indore Museum, and being 
	better, it was helpful to me in solving some points of doubt. 
{2} Denoted by a symbol. 
{3} The sign of anusvāra has been ornamentally treated. 
{4} The first syllable of this word is rather crisped and the consonant of the third appears as s due to a re-
	dundant stroke of the chisel in the beginning. 
{5} Letters which are totally or partially lost at the beginning or end of a line but can easily be made out 
	from the context are put in brackets marked with asterisks. 
{6} The sign of anusvāra over ma of maṃḍita is so lightly engraved as to be seen only on the original. 
{7} These four letters are supplied from the traces left. The akṣara just before the bracket may also have 
	been dhu but should be taken as thu to show that he came from Mathurā, as we also find in No.90, v.11 
	below. 
{8} The reading is not certain and the meaning of the last word is also unknown. Probably adhīẹsaḥ is intended.
	The name of the person is of course mentioned in the fourth foot of the verse. 
{9} The metre of this verse is regulated by the number of syllabic instans (mātrās) and therefore the number of the 
	akṣaras lost here cannot be definitely made out. Similarly, it is not possible to ascertain in certain cases 
	of gaps as to how many of the akṣaras are lost at the end of a line and how many in the beginning of 
	the next line. 
{10} The first two syllables of the compound word appear also as supta. The reading here is uncertain. 
{11} Some other akṣara appears to have been engraved at first and then corrected, by overwriting. The reading 
	 is from the traces left. 
{12} The reading of the bracketed letters is doubtful. 
{13} The construction is grammatically incorrect. 
{14} The reading of both these akṣaras is doubtful. The consonant of the first also appears as s and that 
	 of the second as bh, but this would give no name. 
{15} The gap may be filled in by samanvitaḥ.
{16} This probably signifies Jainism in the present concern. 
{17} The sign of visarga was at first engraved here and later on struck off by a cross mark above.
{18} To restore as sadguṇābdhau. A trace as of the fore part of ṇa is visible at the end of the line. 
{19} All these letters are partly lost and the reading may also have been -ddharaṇapraśasyaṃ(syam). The whole lacunae 
	 be filled in by cakre jinasya duḥkhoddharaṇocitasya, or kṣamsya. 
{20} More than half of v.14 is lost. Kulaka is a number of verses from 5 to 15 and the whole forming one 
	sentence, to be taken here as one part of the inscription. 
{21} Reading from the traces left. 
{22} There are 15 letters in the end, all mutilated. They may be read as -[gnasthi]ḥ sajjanauddharaṇasu(sū)tra-dyeta |
